# TrueProfit Affiliate Resource Hub — Content Guidelines & Instructions

> **Who this file is for:** Anyone or any AI agent writing, reviewing, or contributing content to the TrueProfit Affiliate Resource Hub — internal team, content contributors, or affiliate managers.

---

## 1. What Is the TrueProfit Affiliate Resource Hub?

The Affiliate Resource Hub is a dedicated content space for TrueProfit's affiliate partners. Its purpose is simple: give partners everything they need to understand TrueProfit and promote it effectively — so they can earn commission and do it well.

The people reading this hub are **not** TrueProfit merchants. They are partners who bring new merchants to TrueProfit. That distinction matters a lot for how every piece of content on this hub should be written.

### Who are TrueProfit's affiliate partners?
- Affiliate marketing experts and educators
- Shopify-related community owners (Facebook groups, Discord servers, forums)
- Shopify agencies and service providers (developers, consultants, theme designers)
- eCommerce / dropshipping / print-on-demand course creators
- Shopify-adjacent YouTubers, bloggers, and newsletter writers
- Any relevant individual or brand with an audience of Shopify merchants

Partners may range from those who already know TrueProfit well to those who've never heard of it. Content must work for both ends of that spectrum.

## 2. What Is TrueProfit? (The App Context Every Writer Must Know)

Before writing anything for this hub, understand what TrueProfit actually is — not in a marketing way, but in a practical, real-merchant way.

**TrueProfit is a Shopify profit tracking app.** It connects to a merchant's Shopify store and pulls together all the numbers that Shopify's native dashboard doesn't calculate — like actual net profit after ad spend, COGS, shipping costs, transaction fees, returns, and taxes.

The core problem TrueProfit solves: Shopify shows revenue, but most Shopify merchants — especially those running paid ads or dropshipping — genuinely don't know if they're making money or just making sales. TrueProfit closes that gap by giving them a real-time, accurate profit number.

**Key things TrueProfit tracks:**
- Revenue (synced from Shopify)
- Cost of goods sold (COGS)
- Ad spend (from Meta, Google, TikTok, etc.)
- Shopify and payment processing fees
- Shipping costs
- Custom expenses (handling, VA fees, etc.)
- Returns and refunds
- Net profit — per order, per product, per day/week/month

**Who uses TrueProfit?**
Primarily Shopify merchants who run paid advertising or have significant operational costs — dropshippers, branded print-on-demand stores, DTC brands scaling with ads. Basically: any Shopify seller who needs to know their real margins, not just their Shopify revenue.

**TrueProfit's affiliate program** pays partners a recurring commission for every merchant they refer who becomes a paying subscriber. Details on commission structure, tracking, and payouts belong in dedicated hub sections — but writers should understand the referral dynamic when crafting content.

## 3. Core Content Philosophy

### 3.1 Optimize for Clarity and Coherence First

Every piece of content on this hub — whether it's an explainer, a promotional angle guide, a script, or a ready-to-post example — must be **easy to understand for any partner, regardless of their existing TrueProfit knowledge.**

This means:
- Explain TrueProfit terms and concepts the first time they appear in a given section
- Don't assume partners know Shopify deeply either — use plain language, then layer in precision
- Avoid jargon stacking (e.g., don't say "multi-touch attribution for your CPA tracking on ROAS-positive campaigns" without grounding the reader first)
- If a concept is complex, break it down. Use examples. Make it stick.

### 3.2 No Sales Pitch. Ever.

This is a resource hub for partners — not a landing page for merchants. The content here exists to **inform and equip**, not to sell.

That means:
- Don't write lines like "TrueProfit is the #1 profit tracking solution for Shopify" — that's marketing copy, not a partner resource
- Don't use superlatives or hype language ("game-changing," "revolutionary," "must-have")
- Don't close sections with calls to action pushing partners to buy anything or take urgent steps
- It's fine to say TrueProfit does something well — but frame it from the merchant's lived experience, not as a brand claim

The only acceptable "nudge" in hub content is directing partners toward using the affiliate program itself (e.g., linking to their affiliate dashboard or pointing to a specific resource). Even then, keep it informational.

### 3.3 Partner-Oriented Frame at All Times

Every sentence should be written with the partner's perspective in mind — not the merchant's. The partner is the reader. The merchant is the partner's audience.

Good: *"Your audience is likely full of Shopify sellers who have no idea what their actual profit margins are. This is the core pain TrueProfit addresses."*

Not good: *"TrueProfit helps Shopify sellers understand their profit."* (This addresses the merchant, not the partner promoting TrueProfit to them.)

When writing instructional content, always ask: **"Does this help the partner explain or promote TrueProfit? Or does this sound like a product page?"**

### 3.4 Down-to-Earth Language with Relevant Domain Terms

The tone of this hub is conversational but knowledgeable. Think: an experienced affiliate manager explaining something to a smart peer — not a corporate document, and not a casual text either.

- Use contractions freely (it's, they're, you'll)
- Write shorter sentences when possible
- Use industry-standard terms when relevant: ROAS, COGS, CPM, AOV, dropshipping, print-on-demand, organic content, affiliate link, etc. — but always in context, never as buzzword decoration
- Avoid: corporate filler ("leverage our synergies"), hollow enthusiasm ("super excited to share"), and vague formality ("it is important to note that")

## 4. Tone of Voice Guide

The hub's tone sits in a **formal-casual middle ground**, leaning casual when explaining and leaning slightly more precise when providing structured guidance.

| Situation | Tone Direction |
|---|---|
| Introducing TrueProfit or explaining the app | Casual, clear, conversational |
| Explaining the affiliate program mechanics | Balanced — clear and informative, not dry |
| Providing content angles or promotion strategies | Casual but structured |
| Writing instructional "how-to" content | Slightly more structured, still approachable |
| Hub navigation copy or section intros | Casual, welcoming |
| Ready-to-post social content examples | See Section 6 — these have their own distinct voice rules |

**Voice anchors to keep in mind:**
- Talk *with* partners, not *at* them
- Respect their intelligence and experience
- Be genuinely useful — every sentence should earn its spot

## 5. Structural Consistency Across the Hub

Every route or section within the Affiliate Resource Hub must follow a consistent structural pattern. This helps partners navigate the hub predictably and ensures no matter what page they land on, they immediately know what they're getting.

### 5.1 Standard Section Structure

Each hub page or section should include the following components, in this order:

**1. Section Title**
Clear, descriptive, no clickbait. Examples: *"Understanding TrueProfit: A Quick Brief for Partners"*, *"Content Angles That Work: Reddit Edition"*, *"How Commissions Work"*

**2. Section Summary / "What This Section Covers"**
1–3 sentences at the top of each section, telling partners exactly what they'll get from reading this. No preamble. Just what it is.

**3. Main Content Body**
The actual instructional, informational, or reference material. See Section 6 for specific rules on social content examples embedded here.

**4. Key Takeaways or Quick Reference (when applicable)**
For longer sections, a short summary of the most important points. Use a simple bullet list. Keep each point to one line.

**5. Related Resources or Links (when applicable)**
If the section naturally connects to other parts of the hub or external materials (e.g., affiliate dashboard, TrueProfit's product page, a specific integration doc), link them here. Label clearly.

### 5.2 Heading Hierarchy

- **H1:** Hub name or main page title only (used once per page)
- **H2:** Major sections within a page
- **H3:** Subsections within a major section
- **H4:** Specific sub-points, examples, or content types within a subsection

Don't go deeper than H4. If you need more nesting, restructure the content instead.

### 5.3 Formatting Conventions

- Use **bold** for terms being defined or key points within a paragraph
- Use `code formatting` only for technical strings (e.g., UTM parameters, affiliate link formats)
- Use bullet lists for lists of 3 or more parallel items
- Use numbered lists only for sequential steps
- Use tables for comparisons or structured reference data (see the Tone Guide table above as an example)
- Use blockquotes (`>`) for example copy, sample scripts, or quoted reference text that is pulled out of the main body

## 6. Social-Native Content Examples: Specific Rules

Some sections of the Affiliate Resource Hub include ready-to-post or ready-to-adapt content examples for Reddit and X (formerly Twitter). These examples exist to help partners who want to engage in community-driven promotion — sharing real perspectives, sparking conversations, or publishing useful posts in relevant communities.

**These content examples are fundamentally different from the hub's main content**, and they must be treated and written that way. They exist inside the hub but should not sound like the hub. The goal is for these examples to be credible and effective in their native platforms — meaning they need to sound like they came from a real user, not from an affiliate program's resource doc.

---

### 6.1 Reddit Content Examples

#### The Vibe

Reddit-native content lives and dies by authenticity. Redditors are allergic to corporate language, marketing tone, and anything that smells like a sponsored post or brand promotion. A post that sounds even slightly like an ad will get ignored, downvoted, or called out.

The goal for Reddit content examples is: **sound like a real Shopify merchant or community member who genuinely found something useful — and is sharing it the way Reddit people actually share things.**

#### Core Reddit Voice Rules

- **Raw and direct.** No fluff. Get to the point immediately.
- **First-person, lived-experience framing** whenever possible. Reddit posts that perform are usually "I did X and here's what happened" or "Anyone else dealing with Y?" — not "Here are 5 reasons to use Z."
- **Factual and specific.** Vague posts don't resonate. Real numbers, real scenarios, real friction points — these are what make posts land.
- **No superlatives.** Words like "amazing," "incredible," "life-changing," "must-try" are instant red flags on Reddit.
- **No corporate or marketing vocabulary.** Avoid: "robust solution," "seamless integration," "unlock your potential," "optimize your revenue streams," etc.
- **Skepticism is okay.** Reddit readers relate more to someone who says "I wasn't sure this would help but it actually did" than someone who sounds like a true believer.
- **No calls to action.** Don't end a Reddit example post with "Check it out!" or "Try it here." Either leave it organic, or include a link as a natural mention — not a push.
- **Conversational punctuation and grammar are fine.** Lowercase, casual phrasing, the occasional run-on — this is normal on Reddit and makes content feel authentic.

#### Tone Contrast: What NOT to Write vs. What to Write

**❌ Do not write this (too corporate/sales-y for Reddit):**
> "TrueProfit is an excellent Shopify profit tracking solution that integrates seamlessly with major ad platforms to give merchants a comprehensive view of their actual net profit. If you're running a Shopify store, this is definitely worth exploring."

**✓ Write this instead:**
> "figured out why my store felt profitable but my bank account never showed it — shopify doesn't subtract your ad spend or cogs from what it shows you as 'revenue'. been using an app called trueprofit for a few months and it's the first time i've actually seen a real number at the end of the day. kind of a gut punch when you first see it honestly lol"

### 6.2 X (Twitter/X) Content Examples

#### The Vibe

X content follows similar "no-corporate" rules as Reddit, but the format and dynamic are different. X is faster, more punchy, and often opinion-driven. Good X posts tend to be bold, honest takes — not explainers or story posts.

X posts for this context should feel like something a Shopify seller or ecommerce educator would naturally tweet — a quick observation, a surprising insight, a "hot take" that sparks engagement. Think: the kind of content that gets quote-tweeted with "this."

#### Core X Voice Rules

- **Short and punchy.** X posts should be concise and land their point fast — ideally in the first line.
- **Opinion or insight-led.** Posts that share a perspective or reveal something counterintuitive perform better than posts that just describe a product.
- **Informal but not sloppy.** Unlike Reddit, X has slightly more range for structure — but it should still feel like a person, not a brand account.
- **No sales language.** Same rule as Reddit — no promotional vocabulary, no CTAs that feel like marketing.
- **No hashtag stacking.** One relevant hashtag maximum, if at all. #Shopify is fine when contextually relevant. #ShopifySeller #Ecommerce #ProfitTracking #AppReview stacked together is not.
- **Thread format is acceptable** for slightly longer-form content, but keep each tweet self-contained enough to make sense on its own.
- **Engagement-bait openers work.** Starting with "hot take:", "no one talks about this:", "if you run a shopify store:" — these are native to X and signal that something interesting is coming.

#### Tone Contrast: What NOT to Write vs. What to Write

**❌ Do not write this (too formal and sales-adjacent for X):**
> "Are you tracking your true profitability on Shopify? TrueProfit helps merchants see their actual net profit in real time. Check it out. #Shopify #Ecommerce"

**✓ Write this instead:**
> "shopify revenue is one of the most misleading numbers in ecom. it's just money in — doesn't touch your ad spend, cogs, fees, or anything. your 'best month' might be your worst one. took me an embarrassingly long time to figure this out."

**✓ Or a thread opener:**
> "a lot of shopify sellers are accidentally running at a loss and don't know it. here's why:"


## 7. What Content Belongs in the Hub (and What Doesn't)

### Belongs in the Hub

- **TrueProfit product overview** — what the app does, who it's for, the core problem it solves
- **Audience targeting guidance** — which types of Shopify merchants are the best fit for TrueProfit and why
- **Content angle library** — specific hooks, themes, and narratives that partners can build content around
- **Platform-specific promotion guides** — how to talk about TrueProfit effectively on YouTube, Reddit, email newsletters, Facebook groups, X, etc.
- **Ready-to-adapt content examples** — scripts, post drafts, email templates, captions, with clear labeling of what they are and how to adapt them
- **Affiliate program mechanics** — how tracking works, how commissions are structured, what counts as a valid conversion, payout details
- **Creative assets and materials** — where to find approved screenshots, graphics, and product walkthroughs
- **FAQ for partners** — common questions about the app, the program, and how to answer merchant questions accurately

### Does Not Belong in the Hub

- Generic affiliate marketing education (this hub is TrueProfit-specific, not a general affiliate training course)
- Content that reads like TrueProfit's marketing site copy
- Anything that speaks to merchants directly (this hub is for partners, not end users)
- Hard CTAs pushing partners toward specific actions unrelated to helping them promote effectively

Before submitting or publishing any content to the hub, run through this checklist:

**Voice & Tone**
- [ ] Is this written for the partner, not the merchant?
- [ ] Is the tone conversational but not sloppy?
- [ ] Does it avoid sales language, superlatives, and corporate filler?
- [ ] If this section includes social content examples, do they follow the Reddit/X-specific voice rules in Section 6?

**Clarity**
- [ ] Can someone with zero TrueProfit knowledge understand this?
- [ ] Are all technical terms and app-specific concepts explained or contextualized?

**Structure**
- [ ] Does this section follow the standard structure from Section 5.1?

- [ ] Are social content examples properly labeled with the correct block format?

**Accuracy**
- [ ] Is all information about TrueProfit's features and program accurate and current?
- [ ] Have any claims about what TrueProfit does been verified against actual product documentation?

## 9. Glossary of Terms Used in the Hub

These terms appear throughout hub content. Writers should use them consistently and define them in context the first time they appear within a given section.

| Term | Definition (for hub context) |
|---|---|
| **TrueProfit** | The Shopify profit tracking app that partners are promoting |
| **Merchant** | A Shopify store owner — the end user of TrueProfit, and the partner's target audience |
| **Partner / Affiliate Partner** | The reader of this hub; someone who refers merchants to TrueProfit in exchange for commission |
| **Commission** | The recurring revenue share earned by a partner for each merchant referral who converts to a paid plan |
| **Affiliate link** | The unique tracking URL each partner uses to refer merchants |
| **COGS** | Cost of goods sold — the direct cost per product unit, a key input in TrueProfit's profit calculations |
| **Ad spend** | Money spent on paid advertising (Meta, Google, TikTok, etc.) — TrueProfit syncs this to calculate real profit |
| **Net profit** | Revenue minus all costs (COGS, ad spend, fees, shipping, returns, etc.) — the core metric TrueProfit shows |
| **ROAS** | Return on ad spend — a common metric merchants use; TrueProfit helps contextualize it against real profit |
| **Shopify fees / transaction fees** | Platform and payment processing costs Shopify charges merchants; TrueProfit accounts for these |
| **Dropshipping** | A Shopify business model where merchants sell products fulfilled directly by suppliers — a key TrueProfit use case |
| **Print-on-demand (POD)** | A Shopify model where products are made and shipped per order — another key TrueProfit use case |
| **Content angle** | A specific hook or narrative approach a partner can use to talk about TrueProfit in their content |
| **Organic content** | Non-paid content — blog posts, Reddit posts, YouTube videos, community posts — as opposed to paid ads |
